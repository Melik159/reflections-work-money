Thus, in order to increase the wealth of any economic system, it's imperative to

to decrease the price of the Products (goods and services) put on the market. In fact,
if yesterday a 1$ coin made it possible to buy 1 kg of pasta and today the price per

to increase by 11.1% since 1$ consents to the purchase of this extra pasta (1 / 9 =

price reduction to purchase another good or service.
However, all conventional economic doctrines consider Money to be that which
circulates from the consumer to the supplier in exchange for a good or service, which
circulates in the opposite direction. As a result, they are always obliged to conceive,
imagine, and construct “initial endowments” that allow consumers to acquire goods
and services. A contrario, following this new approach, it's possible to write :

The Money is what a consumer holds in addition after a price drop.
It is only later that this Money will circulate from the consumer to the supplier in
exchange for a Product (good or service). Thus, this new definition captures how
these initial endowments could be generated and, as a result, wealth and increased
purchasing power.
Genesis of the money 
To achieve this price reduction it's normally necessary and indispensable to
increase productivity in order to manufacture the good or service as quickly as
possible (caeteris paribus). However, this increase in productivity necessarily
requires the execution of additional Work (defined here as energy and not as a task, a
job, a labour, something to be done).
Consequently, it's therefore possible to exhibit the following phenomenological
sequence:

Additional work of the supplier ⇒ increased productivity ⇒ price decline
⇒ increase of monetary unit ⇒ creation of money ⇒ additional money of
the consumer.
It should be noted that this price reduction should not be used to feed dividends,
salaries, profits or anything else. Indeed, in this case and as already mentioned, this
